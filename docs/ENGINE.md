# Engine architecture — build 3

The user requires a sound engine written from scratch, with an entirely new architecture, and asked build 3 to recreate the Pulsar-23's whole design rather than its four oscillators. Build 2's per-track engine with four fixed modulation cables is superseded.

## Boundary

`firmware/engine/p23_engine.c` includes only `p23_engine.h` and `p23_math.h`. It links alone (`tests/host/engine_standalone.c`). The frontend (`engine_control.c`, `tracks.c`, `ui.c`) translates sessions into `p23_patch` (circuit knobs and envelope), `p23_route` (VOL, pan, FX send, mute, duck), `p23_panel` (clock, SHAOS, LFO, FX, distortion, attenuators, CV) and 32 `p23_cable` joins, and forwards keys, sequencer and MIDI gates plus looper sensor events.

The looper-recorder lives in the engine because its clocks can be any patched signal at audio rate. Its tapes (6 channels × 4 banks × 12 288 two-bit cells, 72 KB) are volatile, as on the hardware.

## Signal model and patchbay

- Pins carry voltages, 3277 units per volt (10 V = 32 768). CV and gates are 0–10 V; module, noise and FX outputs carry a +5 V bias, as on the instrument. EXT, MIX IN and the FX aux inputs are AC-coupled.
- A cable joins two pins. Pins joined (directly or through other cables) form a net. A net's value is the mean of its active outputs; GND and +10 V weigh eight times, so they dominate. Inputs that are also outputs (TRIG, CLK, SHAOS DATA, switch and component terminals) read the net without their own contribution.
- Envelope trigger inputs take the strongest voltage on the TRIG net (the manual's 2 V / 5 V / 7 V example).
- A patched CLK, LR CLK, SHAOS CLK or S/H pin replaces the internal clock (Schmitt thresholds 3 V master, 2 V elsewhere). Grounding a looper CLK stops that channel.
- The diode conducts anode → cathode (0.6 V drop) and clamps the anode net to the cathode + 0.6 V when the cathode is driven. Capacitors in series pass the other side's changes, decaying with 10 ms (0.1 µF) or 1 s (10 µF); with one side on GND they make the other net an RC low-pass.
- Every net is evaluated from the previous sample's outputs, so a patch path has one sample of delay regardless of order. Nodes with no cable into them render a whole block at a time (their gate is the block's, ≤ 0.73 ms resolution); nodes with a cable into an input, or a patched TRIG, run sample by sample. Silent nodes rest: their timebase advances a block at once and their per-block preparation is skipped.

## Modules

- **Clock**: the FM-1 has no clock settings: it always sends INT with no MOD amount (`p23_global_from_frontend`), so the clock is the session tempo; the engine still supports EXT and MOD × AMT. INT is the session tempo as 32nd-note ticks (the Pulsar's 128-tick, four-bar loop), × 2^(MOD × AMT × 3 octaves / 10 V). It runs with the FM-1 transport and restarts at play. EXT follows the CLK pin. Dividers are a 7-stage counter, high for the first half of each cycle, all rising at the loop start.
- **Looper-recorder**: positions in 1/96 tick, advanced by the master clock or a channel CLK pin (period measured, upsampled, held at the next tick until the edge). ADD overdubs (max), DEL erases, DEL + ADD punches in; PLAY + DEL mutes playback; L / M re-scale playback while held. Velocity levels are 4 V, 7 V and 10 V. Bank copy runs while held, on REC channels. Quantize moves each note's start to the nearest 16th and stops the looper. The keys are the track's ADD sensor; the LOOPER page also gives ADD and DEL keys.
- **Envelopes**: linear attack 0.3 ms … ~5 s toward the gate level, exponential release 2 ms … ~10 s, sustain while gated. `p23_patch.perc` makes one percussive: it falls once its attack peaks, the gate held or not, until the next strike; a held gate then costs nothing once the sound has died. The FM-1 sets it on every sound and caps REL at 72 (a ~0.22 s time constant).
- **BD**: an integrator/comparator triangle core (pitch from the note). A pitch modulator is charged at each strike: PITCH sets depth (up to ~4 octaves) and decay (3–120 ms). MOD is linear, up to 60 Hz/V at full AMT. WTF? injects current into the modulator, OMG! into the core. DRIVE is a gain of 0.5–24 into a soft knee: triangle, then sine, then square, level-compensated. The core free-runs between hits (STAB resets it).
- **BASS**: a table-free recursive DCO, y = sin(φ + β·ȳ), SHAPE raising β toward saw, then crossfading to a band-limited square; phase modulation from MOD × AMT; WARP an asymmetric loaded saturator; EXT; a trapezoidal state-variable LPF whose band integrator saturates (self-oscillates at high Q). DCO: the note, glide, bend and 1 V/oct CV IN; PRC: an impulse-and-noise-excited array of three two-pole resonators (input scaled by the square root of the bandwidth so they ring evenly) (harmonic when SHAPE is low, a detuned cluster when high; WARP drives and cross-couples them; AMT's MOD ducks the output), then the same LPF.
- **SD**: pink noise and a pitched spectral noise (an oscillator following the note, frequency-swept by band-limited noise; linear MOD × AMT), MIX, the clap (1–4 bursts 4–19 ms apart, from ADD, LR playback at full level, or velocity ≥ 113; never from a patched TRIG), EXT, resonant BPF, its level held across Q.
- **Further modes** (`p23_patch.mode`; written for this firmware, not the instrument's): BD HAMMER, the triangle core and pitch modulator folded 1–9 times over (SHAPE), a low-passed noise rumble that outlasts the strike (longer with STAB), a click, then a hard asymmetric clip; BASS GRIND, a sine whose phase its own overdriven filter's low-pass state pushes (SHAPE), ring-modulated by noise (WARP), hard-clipped into the resonant LPF; SD WALL, white noise held at a falling sample rate (AMT, 44.1 kHz … ~340 Hz), bits dropped (MIX, up to 12), chopped at about 0.5–31 Hz (CLAP), then the BPF and a hard clip (no clap in this mode); HHT STATIC, impulses of random sign as dense as WARP and arcs (short noise bursts) through the resonant HPF; BD SKIN, a struck fundamental and (1,1) membrane mode (× 1.593) in state-variable resonators, swept by the pitch modulator, SHAPE the second mode and the head's saturation, STAB a stick click; BASS PLUCK, a Karplus–Strong string (a line one period long in a 2048-word per-node buffer, linearly interpolated, loop damped by SHAPE, sustained by WARP, excited for one period by noise as bright as SHAPE and by EXT); BASS FM, a two-operator ping (ratio by SHAPE from 1, 1.41, 2, 2.76, 3.5, 4.2, 5.4, 7; WARP the index, which falls in 2–90 ms with REL); SD TONE, two body modes (× 1, × 1.47, struck with a short downward bend, a fifth above the note) mixed against filtered noise by MIX; SD WIRE, the SD noise through a comb two octaves above the note (feedback with Q; the BPF's Q fixed); HHT METAL, six square oscillators at the 808's ratios three octaves below the noise clock, summed or ring-modulated in pairs with WARP; HHT SHAKE, noise in grains a few milliseconds long, as dense as WARP, one at every strike.
- **HHT**: white noise resampled at a clock following the note, WARP an expander |x|^1…4 toward sparse grains, EXT, resonant HPF.
- **SHAOS**: one 8-stage register. Feedback Q1⊕Q8 (63), Q3⊕Q8 (217 = 7 × 31) or ¬Q8 (a 16-step twisted ring; DATA overwrites the input). The reducible feedback also has short cycles; the emulation leaves them, so the published lengths hold. 1-bit DIR = Q1, 2-bit DIR = Q2–Q3, 1-bit S/H = Q4, 3-bit S/H = Q5–Q7.
- **LFO**: LOW 0.05–13 Hz, MID 0.5–256 Hz, HI 10 Hz–5 kHz; shape falling saw → triangle → rising saw; square 50 %; SYNC; MOD × AMT (5 octaves at 10 V).
- **FX**: an FV-1-like processor with 32K words of memory at a 32 768 Hz clock that CLK MOD × MOD slows up to 7× (delay times, pitch and bandwidth scale with it; converter filters follow). Delay (1 ms … 0.59 s at full clock) with a BPF (TUNE), a second tap (TUNE), or a ±1-octave shifter (TUNE) in the feedback. Reverb: three diffusers and two cross-fed loops of an all-pass and a damped line; in PCH a shifter opposite to the delay's. FB sets delay feedback and reverb decay. MAD! in BPF / PCH: signal-dependent delay reads, feedback past unity with clipping, random pitch jumps; in DBL: stereo outputs.
- **Mix and output**: OUT is post-envelope, pre-VOL; the FX send is post-VOL (on the instrument it is pre-VOL; here VOL, the sound's VOL and the track level turn a track down in the FX too). The FX send goes to the delay or reverb (DLY/REV switch). Mix + FX returns + MIX IN → parallel distortion (high-gain asymmetric clipper, DRIVE / MIX) → DC blocking → 6 dB of gain into a soft clip → output (the FM-1 writes it at full scale: `main.c` OUT_SHIFT 8, where it was −6 dBFS). The output is meant loud: hot mixes saturate in the soft clip rather than wrap. The FM-1's pan is kept; the Pulsar bus is mono.

## Pitch, voice drive, routes, macros (build 3, revision 2)

- **Pitch from the note.** TUNE is gone from the panel. BD sounds two octaves below the note, the SD spectral noise four above, the HHT noise clock six above, BASS at the note (glide, bend, CV IN). `knob[0]` is the recipe's offset in half semitones.
- **WRECK** (`p23_patch.fold`, `ring`, `ring_freq`, `crush`, `bits`), after the overdrive and before the VCA route: a triangle fold driven up to ~5×, a ring modulator crossfaded in (its own sine at 20 Hz–5 kHz, independent of the note), a sample hold every 1–16 samples, and up to 12 bits dropped. Bypassed when all are 0; a few instructions a sample each.
- **STUTTER** (`p23_set_stutter`, `p23_stutter_hold`), apart from the FX, after the FX returns and MIX IN and before the distortion: the output (a quarter of L + R, mono) is written to its own 32768-sample memory (0.74 s) while nothing is held. Held, on the next 32nd tick while the INT clock runs (at once otherwise), the last slice (1/32 .. 1/4 or a triplet of the tempo, at most the memory) is frozen and read at RATE (0.25–4×); at each pass DECAY scales it (to half per pass at most), DRIFT multiplies the rate by up to ±½ octave, ROLL halves the slice every 1–8 passes (down to 256 samples), ALT turns it, RND draws a way and ×½ / ×1 / ×2; the loop's edges fade over 48 samples; MIX crossfades it over the output (at 64 a sample, ~12 ms). Let go, it fades back; the DC blocking and distortion after it run on what it gives. A break's STUT layer drives the same stutter with its own settings. The engine takes values only; the frontend resolves the page's RND settings (SLICE, RATE, MODE, DECAY) into a value drawn at each press before it asks for the hold (`engine_control.c`, `stut_pick`). The page's WILD is the engine's `P23_ST_RND`.
- **Voice overdrive** (`p23_patch.drive`, SHIFT + KNOB 1), between the circuit and its VCA: a one-pole pre-emphasis (up to +6 dB above ~1.2 kHz), gain 0.6–12, an asymmetric cubic clipper (3v − v³)/2 whose bias sags with the output level (the colour changes as it is pushed), a de-emphasis low-pass from 16 kHz down to 5 kHz, and level compensation 1 / (1.5 · min(gain, 4/3)). Below 1/4 it crossfades in from dry. It bypasses at 0. Roughly a dozen multiplies a sample.
- **Routes** (`p23_patch.mod[4]`): a source pin × depth / 127 into EXT, MOD1–5, DRIVE (gain × (1 + v/10 V)) or VCA (× (1 + v/10 V); negative depth ducks). Biased sources (track OUT, NOISE, FX outputs) pass their swing only. The track's own OUT / ENV are stored symbolically and resolved to the track the sound is on. Sources a route reads (SHAOS, LFO, utilities, track OUT / ENV) are computed even when no cable uses them.
- **Route timing (approximation, for load).** A routed voice still renders a block at a time: every other source is recorded sample by sample during one block and read by the voices during the next, so it arrives 0.73 ms (32 samples) late; the track's own ENV is computed for the whole block first and followed sample by sample; a voice whose route reads its own OUT runs sample by sample on CPU0. Sources are recorded only while a node reading them sounds (a node waking reads none for its first block); SHAOS and the LFO read by routes alone then keep time a block at once. EXT routes follow every sample; MOD, DRIVE and VCA routes every fourth (11 kHz); a filter cutoff or HHT clock moved only by routes is recomputed every eighth sample (every second / fourth with a cable). SHAOS and the LFO, read by routes alone, step every fourth sample. At most 12 distinct sources are recorded; further ones are not heard.
- **Macros** live in the frontend (`engine_control.c` `MACRO_MAP`): M1–M4 and M8 move up to four parameters each, bipolar about the recipe (64), and scale the routes that name them; M5 is DRIVE, M6 the FX send, M7 ATTACK.
- **Recipes** (`gen.c`): 147 percussive families over the 16 profiles (the four circuits in their modes) (no drones; SHAOS and the clock dividers never on a pitch: `pitch_dst`, `stepped_src`; each family's VOL measured to level its hits, `FAM_VOL`), each a circuit setting, envelope, drive, send, spread, and up to four routes with up to three candidate sources each; drawing adds spread to every knob, picks sources and depths, and with probability ½ patches a further route from the circuit's pool on M8. The default SHAOS rate is now about 3 Hz so its routes step audibly.

## Fidelity limits

The sources are the SOMA manual v1.2, the Synthdawg Pulsar-23 Notebook and public reviews, forum posts and the FAQ (research notes are local). None give component values, transfer curves, the BD/BASS firmware, the SD noise circuit, the HHT WARP circuit, the SHAOS taps (the 8-stage structure is inferred from the lengths) or the FX programs (the FX chip is unconfirmed). Every range above is this engine's choice. WTF?/OMG! are current injections into modelled nodes, not the real bend points. Skin conductivity, impedance interactions and physical CV pins are approximated by the net rules. No acoustic equivalence is claimed.

## Two cores

The block runs in parts (`p23_process_begin`, `p23_process_part(0 / 1)`, `p23_process_end`). Begin (CPU0) reads the panel, takes every node's gate and prepares it, rests the silent nodes and shares the sounding block-rendered ones between parts 0 and 1 by a rough cost. CPU1 renders part 1 while CPU0 renders part 0 (`tracks.c` `mix_block`, through the inherited `audio_worker.h` mailbox). The parts write separate buses and share no state; end (CPU0) joins them, then runs the patch, the cabled nodes, the FX and the output sample by sample. With CPU1 held or retired ("1 CORE"), CPU0 renders both parts; a CPU1 fault loses one block of its part's voices. The standalone test checks that the parts in either order render bit for bit as one core does.

The voice renders in passes: the envelope, the circuit, the overdrive and VCA route, then the strip, each with its state in locals. The once-a-block code (the panel, the routes' table, every node's preparation, the gates) runs from flash, leaving RAM to the per-sample code. Each FX channel rests on its own (the DLY / REV switch feeds one). The FX processor rests after 6200 silent blocks (4.5 s, longer than its longest repeat at the 7× clock stretch) with nothing sent to it and its outputs below −66 dB, and wakes with the next send. The looper is advanced only while its clock moves (the master running, or a patched clock). The frontend recomputes a track's patch only when its sound, its locks or an LFO on it changed, a note was played, or every 32 blocks.

## Device load (this revision)

Measured with the telemetry build: 25 % idle (the frontend about 8 % of it); all six tracks on 16ths 39 % mean, 48 % peak with the start's kit and 49 % / 58 % with a heavy one (feedback kick, SCREAM, SNARE HOWL, crash, tabla, sitar), no late buffers. A continuously retriggered voice: 9–19 % of CPU0 by family (mean 14 %, the frontend's per-note work included); own-OUT feedback sounds are the dearest (they run sample by sample). Rendering on CPU0 alone or waiting for CPU1 more gently measured the same per voice.

## Device load (before this revision)

Measured with the telemetry build, 44.1 kHz, the frontend included (about 8 %). Sessions without a save draw their sounds at boot, so the load moves with the routes drawn; one boot: 36 % idle; 59 % with one sound on 16ths and the transport running (the FX awake); 61 % (peak 68 %) with all six tracks on 16ths; 89 % with six routed voices held, no late buffers. Before this work the same kind of session was 83 % idle and 143 % with six voices held. Routes remain the largest per-voice cost (about half of it); the voice overdrive about a tenth. Cabled nodes run sample by sample on CPU0 and cost more; dense patches are not measured.
